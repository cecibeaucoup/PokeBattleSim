Every pokemon has the following stats:

Power: Reflects a pokemon's ability to deal damage. Every rank in power adds two damage dice, and adds one more per tier.
Toughness: Reflects a pokemon's ability to take damage. Every rank in toughness reduces incoming damage by one, plus one additional point for every tier.
Speed: Speed determines how fast a pokemon acts, as well as adding two hit dice per rank and one dodge per minus-rank tier, then another dodge for plus-rank tier.
Stamina: How long a pokemon can go on. Stamina affects how much effort a pokemon can exert without tiring out, as well as its Health Points. Gain two HP per rank, as well as one for every letter grade.

Every pokemon has the following skills:

Aim: Your ability to attack accurately at range. Every rank makes four hit dice count as automatic successes. S-rank Aim lowers the success threshold of a hit dice for ranged attacks by 1.
Brawl: Your ability to attack accurately up-close. Every rank makes four hit dice count as automatic successes. S-rank Brawl lowers the success threshold of a hit dice for melee attacks by 1.
Efficiency: Represents the amount of normal actions a pokemon can take before becoming more exhausted.
Evasion: Makes pokemon better at avoiding attacks – each rank grants +2 or 20% to a pokemon's dodge value, whichever is biggest.
Friendship: How close a trainer and a pokemon are – unlocks options.

HP: When the number of damage successes exceed a Pokemon's Defense value, the difference generates HP damage. A pokemon faints when it runs out of HP – and receives -1 Toughness at 75%, 50% and 25% HP. HP is tied to Stamina, weight, and nature.
Energy: Every pokemon has 4/4 energy levels. Being lower than full energy gives a -1 penalty on Power per energy level, -1 to Speed per 2 energy levels, and all maluses are doubled at 0 energy levels. Energy levels lower when the pokemon exerts itself, based on the pokemon's Stamina and Efficiency.

On Combat:

Combat will have a mechanical component and a descriptive component. On the mechanical component, every move utilizes some attributes and some traits and rolls them against the opponent. If the hit beats the dodge and the damage beats the toughness, then the move succeeds and removes HP. Every action causes exhaustion to a certain point.

On the descriptive end, actions are not simply attacks - every action will require a battle plan. Every battle plan will consist of two moves - one for an offensive situation and one for a defensive situation. The plans will also have a descriptor word, which reflects the overall feel of the plan's strategy. Different-natured pokemon will have different descriptors - Rafflesia, being a Gentle pokemon, may have access to descriptors such as Patient, Judicious or Precise and may not have access to descriptors such as Raring, Cruel or Reckless. Proper use of descriptors will give bonuses to actions, while choosing the wrong descriptor may instead incur penalties.

Adding to this is the final part of the plan, the write-in. Plan bases, in the form of move-descriptor combos, will be offered by the QM and may be modified, but every plan must include a write-in describing how the situation is handled, and the actual plan in fluff. The planning may also grant bonuses or penalties, based on whether or not it works as based on a d20 dice roll with bonuses or minuses depending on the description itself.

There is no physical/special split in this quest. This leads to a single defensive stat (Toughness) and a single attacking stat (Power). The split is instead about ranged and melee moves, which use Aim and Brawl respectively to power up themselves and their accuracy.

A pokemon attacking with a move of its type gains the effects of Same Type Attack Bonus (STAB), which makes 1/6th of its damage dice be automatic successes.

Movement Types are as following: Fly, Levitate and other airborne movement types will grant +2 dodge against Aim moves and +4 dodge against Brawl moves from enemies who cannot employ another airborne movement type, so long as the user is flying. Swim movement types will grant +3 to dodge while in water. Dig movement makes the user gain +4 defense against single-target moves but makes it so every tremor-move hits automatically. Teleport movement type adds a +2 bonus result to all Dodge actions. Jump movement without an alternative movement type gives a -2 to dodge.

Leveling is based on Trainer level - there are no pokemon levels. Pokemon are leveled narratively, and depend on ther Friendship value to unlock access to Traits, move tutoring options, and other benefits.

Teaching a pokemon a Trait costs 1000 800 EXP. A pokemon has access to Traits as determined by its friendship. Traits can only be taken once per pokemon.

TRAITS
Ace: This pokemon stands above the rest of its species. It has +3 to one attribute and +1 to the other three.
Prodigy: This pokemon is particularly skilled in certain areas. Subtract one from a skill, then add three points to skills of your choice. Limit of maximum two points per skill. A skill may be raised only up to rank 5 through this Trait. You cannot subtract points from skills that would put them under rank 0)
Species Trait: This pokemon is a prodigious specimen of its species, and unlocks a trait unique to that pokemon species.
Extra Ability: This pokemon learns another ability.
Specialist: This pokemon becomes more proficient at using one of its types, increasing the STAB bonus from 1/6 to 1/4. It also increases the accuracy of status moves of the type, which gain +3 accuracy.
Mitigation: This pokemon reduces the effect of one of its weaknesses by half
Focus: This pokemon subtracts four points from anywhere in its attributes, then adds six points to its attributes. Maximum 4 points added to a single attribute.

Signature Move: This pokemon is especially proficient at the utilization of a specific move. If not of its type, the move gains STAB as if it was of the pokemon's type and is affected by Specialist. If the move IS of the pokemon's type, the user lowers the exhaustion caused by using the move by one stage.
Combination: The pokemon chooses two moves - it can now use that specific combination as a single move. Signature Moves can be combined with the moves chosen for combination, for no additional cost.
Support Mastery: The Pokémon chooses a support move - a number of times equal to Efficiency/2 times per battle (minimum 1), the user can use the move without expending an action. Furthermore, the effects of moves used this way are improved.

MOVES
A trainer can teach its pokemon C-rank moves or lower, as well as moves from a rank based on the following list: (TL 4=B, TL 8=A, TL 10=S). A trainer can also train pokemon in moves known by their previous evolutions, so long as the evolution was not stone-based. Certain tutors may be able to teach moves to pokemon that they would otherwise not learn. Certain pokemon may break these rules with specific moves.

D-Rank - 100 EXP.
C-Rank - 200 EXP.
B-Rank - 500 EXP
A-Rank - 1000 EXP
S-Rank - 2500 EXP

The Boosting Clarity Rule: Boosting move durations in the Movedex are those for their Defensive use. Using a boosting move offensively makes it last twice as long.

The Five-Hit Crit Rule: Five hit moves generate crits in every accuracy dice normally, up to 10 hit dice. If a five-hit move generates more than 10 crit dice, then the amount of crit dice generated is divided by five.

The Sonic Interference Rule: Sonic moves halve the effect of any Suppress against them that is not from a Sonic move, and can only be parried with other Sonic moves.

There are four different defensive actions, not keywords.

Defend, which rolls dice equal to your Toughness to increase the user's Defense - and is in the plans with Focus Energy.

Dodge/Parry - which rolls dice equal to your Dodge to increase your Dodge. Shown with Double Kick.

Counterattack - which is firing an attack to deal damage while the opponent is open due to their own offensive. Shown with Ice Beam.

Suppress - which is firing an attack with no intention to hit, trying to obstruct the opponent and thus cause them to miss their own attack. The example we had for its use is firing a Water Pulse at a hopefully-approaching beedrill to obstruct its fly path.

Pokemon can initiate a Grapple on every maneuver that employs the use of a grabbing appendage to grasp at their opponent somehow - for example, moves such as Seismic throw or vine-using moves. For the vast majority of Pokemon, grappling cannot be done at range - it can only be utilized up close. Certain moves, like Vine Whip, allow their users to utilize moves at-range after the vines, or other appendages, are deployed.

A Pokemon initiates a Grapple by starting a maneuver. In Offensive uses, this would be simply an attack. With defensive uses, a Grapple can only be attempted on Defend, Counterattack, or Suppress actions. The grapple's success is determined by the success of the attached action - if a Counterattack, the counter must hit and deal damage. If a Defend, the opponent's attack must deal no damage outside of crits. If a Suppress, the opponent's move must miss. If this conditions are met, or the user hits with the grappling attack on the Offense, then the opponent is grappled.

The grappler always goes before the grappled pokemon in initiative, and the grappled pokemon cannot utilize dodge actions while it remains grappled. Moves with priority have their priority nullified by this effect. At the end of every round after the first, the grappler and the grappled pokemon make an opposed damage dice roll that accounts for Brawl - or Aim, if the grapple is ranged. If the grappler wins the roll-off, the opponent's pokemon remains grappled. If the grappled pokemon wins the roll-off, it frees itself from the grapple. While maintaining a grapple, the grappler has a -2 penalties to all actions against pokemon that it is not grappling but a +2 to hit against any pokemon that it has grappled. The grappled pokemon has a -2 penalty to all actions not against its grappler. If an opponent escapes a grapple, it cannot be grappled again for one turn.

A Type Specialist is a trainer that has, through training many pokemon that utilize a specific Type, learned insights that allow them to train their pokemon to use that type in more effective, efficient and powerful ways as well as to influence their growth towards that type. The abilities of a Type Specialist increase the more pokemon of a specific type they have on their team and have raised to at least 1 friendship. Any pokemon with a Species Trait that grants them characteristics of a specific type (STAB, Resistances) or adds moves of the type, also makes it count as a pokemon of the Type for the sake of progressing Type Specialist and receiving bonuses.

A trainer must have at least two pokemon of a specific type to qualify for a Type Specialist trait. When a trainer becomes a Type Specialist, they become able to Tutor C-rank moves of the type and also become able to teach D-Rank moves of the type free of cost.

At 2 Pokemon (Rank 2): The Type Specialist gains a bonus to Damage rolls equal to 1/2 the Type Specialist's rank on all moves of the Type used by their pokemon.
At 3 Pokemon (Rank 3): The Type Specialist gains a bonus to Accuracy rollls equal to 1/2 of the Type Specialist's rank on all moves of the Type used by their pokemon.
At 4 Pokemon (Rank 4): The Type Specialist becomes able to Tutor B-Rank moves of their type. Teaching C-Rank moves of the type is half-cost for the Type Specialist. The Damage Bonus granted by the Rank 2 Type Specialist tier becomes equal to the Type Specialist's Rank. Any pokemon the trainer trains has a 50% Chance to manifest a Species Trait related to the associated type, when investigating Species Traits - even without associated ancestry or influence.
At 5 Pokemon (Rank 5): The Type Specialist reduces the critical threshold of all moves of the associated type by 1/2 of the Type Specialist's Rank, and gains a bonus to Defensive actions of their associated Type moves also equivalent to 1/2 of their Type Specialist rank.
At 6 Pokemon (Rank 6): The Type Specialist becomes able to tutor A-Rank moves of their type. They also teach B-Rank moves of their type at half cost. Any pokemon the trainer trains has a 75% Chance to manifest a Species Trait related to the associated type, when investigating Species Traits - even without associated ancestry or influence. All of the Type Specialist's pokemon resist the Type Specialist's type one stage further.
At 7 Pokemon (Rank 7): All moves of the Type Specialist's type used by the Type Specialist's pokemon act as if one Rank lower for the purposes of Energy rolls. The Type Specialist may tutor some associated Type moves to pokemon that do not normally learn them. A Type Specialist can Stunt moves of the associated type without incurring an energy penalty, so long as both moves are of the Type Specialist's type.
At 8 Pokemon (Rank 8): The Type Specialist becomes able to tutor S-Rank moves of their type. They also teach A-Rank moves of their type at half cost. Any pokemon the trainer trains is guaranteed to manifest a Species Trait option related to the associated Type, when investigating Species Traits - even without associated ancestry or influence. A Type Specialist becomes able to Stunt moves of the associated type without energy penalty - even with moves not of the associated type. A Type Specialist also unlocks a specific Type Specialist Capstone Ability.

Type Specialists Capstones
Normal: Any Normal-type moves automatically succeed at 1/4th of their Damage Dice (Before STAB) as well as spend energy as if 1 Rank lower. Normal-type moves become able to strike Ghost-types.
Fighting: The Pokemon becomes able to Combo, which lets them Stunt an additional Fighting-type B-rank or lower move to a stunt for free. The Pokemon can Combo a number of times per battle equal to their highest Skill. Pokemon can spend multiple Combo charges in a single stunt, equal to their Efficiency rank.
Flying: All pokemon that do not have a Fly speed gain a Fly speed. Pokemon that already have a Fly speed now apply their Brawl Fly Dodge to all attacks and instead of losing Fly speed due to effects, they go down to their Aim Fly Dodge. Flying-type moves multiply their Accuracy Dice by 1.5.
Poison: Any effect that would inflict Poison now inflicts Toxic Poison. All effects that inflict Toxic Poison now start with one additional stack. All instances that would apply one Toxic Poison stack now apply two. Moves against Poisoned opponents gain 1 tier of STAB.
Ground: All Pokemon without a Dig movement type gain one. All pokemon that already have Dig movement now apply their dodge bonus against all attacks. Dig movement type now does not incur any penalties, nor does it allow certain moves to automatically hit. Groundsource moves such as Earthquake now strike airborne opponents, albeit at half damage. Any Ground-type moves now apply a -1 stacking penalty to accuracy rolls for Efficiency rounds.
Rock: All Rock-type moves have a 25% chance to flinch, which accumulates with every consecutive hit and resets on flinch. If the opponent flinches, the user gains +1 Toughness for Efficiency turns and resets the flinch chance down to 25%. Rock-type Pokemon gain the ability to apply Sandstorm as per the move for free, once per battle. The specialist's Pokemon take no damage from Sandstorm.
Bug: Pokemon becomes able to Swarm, which lets them give the Double Hit quality to all moves in a single action. It can do this a number of times equal to their highest skill. Resisted Bug-type moves used by pokemon are instead resisted one less stage.
Ghost: All pokemon that do not have a Levitate speed gain a Levitate speed. Pokemon that already have a Levitate speed now apply their Brawl Levitate Dodge to all attacks and instead of losing Levitate speed due to effects, they go down to their Aim Levitate Dodge. If a Ghost-type move causes an opponent to drop a Health Threshold, toss a coin. On heads, the opponent also loses one energy level.
Steel: All Resisted types are resisted one further stage. This cannot make a pokemon Immune to a type.
Fire: All Fire-type moves that deal damage inflict a Toughness penalty equal to 1/4th the dealt damage (minimum 1) for Efficiency turns. All moves roll 1.25x their damage dice against Burned targets. Pokemon of this type gain the ability to freely activate harsh sunlight as per the move Sunny Day once per battle.
Water: All pokemon gain a Swim speed. Pokemon that already have a Swim speed now incur no penalties for not having a swimming place and gain +2 Speed in water. Water-type pokemon become able to set up rain as per the move Rain Dance for free, once per battle.
Grass: All Grass-type moves heal the user for 1/10th of the damage dealt (minimum 1) on top of any other healing sources. The user can also initiate ranged Grapples with every Grass-type move, if the move deals damage. Grass-type pokemon become able to set up Grassy Terrain as per the move, once per battle.
Electric: Pokemon now automatically add 1/4th of their Speed as bonus Accuracy dice on all accuracy rolls. Pokemon also add 1/4th of their Speed as bonus Damage Dice autosuccesses against Paralyzed targets. Electric-type Pokemon become able to set up Electric Terrain as per the move for free, once per battle.
Psychic: All Psychic-type moves now hit automatically against Confused targets. Confused targets also switch their Brawl and Aim for the purpose of accuracy dice to contact and ranged moves. Psychic-type pokemon become able to set up Psychic Terrain as per the move for free, once per battle.
Ice: Double the Freeze chance of all Freeze instances from moves. Every time an Ice-type move deals damage, it also causes the target to lose 1 speed for Efficiency rounds. Multi-hit moves apply this effect multiple times. Ice-type pokemon become able to set up Hail as per the move for free, once per battle. The trainer's pokemon become immune to Hail damage.
Dragon: Dragon-type moves are one stage more Effective. For Dragon-type pokemon, this also applies to all neutral hits.
Dark: The Counterattack action now causes the user to gain +4 dodge for the action's duration and increase the STAB rank of the used move by 1. Social keyword bonuses are doubled. If the user successfully deals more damage with a Counterattack than the associated Attack, the enemy loses 1 Power for Efficiency rounds, stackable.
Fairy: Fairy-type moves that generate a crit also cause the opponent to lose 1 Power for Efficency rounds, stackable. Pokemon become able to freely use Brawl or Aim for all their moves. Fairy-type Pokemon become able to set up Misty Terrain as per the move for free, once per battle.
