Trainer Level: When two trainers are of mismatched levels, the higher-leveled trainer gains a +1 bonus to hit dice and damage dice and the lower-leveled trainer gains a -1 penalty for every level of difference. The bonus / penalty magnitude is capped at twice the Fellowship level of the advantaged pokemon, and uncapped at Fellowship 5.

Attributes:
- Power: Determines the pokemon's attack power, and how much damage it deals. Each point of power grants two damage dice, and one additional dice per full letter grade.
- Speed: Determines the pokemon's speed, which sets action order, accuracy and evasion. Each point of speed grants two hit dice, one point of dodge, and one point of Priority on all of the user's actions.
- Toughness: Determines the pokemon's resistance to damage. Each point of toughness grants one point of defense, and one additional point per full letter grade.
- Stamina: Determines the pokemon's ability to endure damage, and the size of its energy reserve for throwing out powerful moves. Each point of stamina grants two wounds, or three wounds for S-Rank tiers and four for EX-Rank, and increases the number of moves a pokemon can use without tiring out.

Skills:
- Aim: Determines how good a pokemon is at hitting its target when attacking from range. Each level of aim turns five hit dice into guaranteed successes when attacking from range. If the number of automatic successes exceeds the number of dice rolls, the excess are converted to hit dice. For each hit success that exceeds the opponent's dodge, there is a 50% chance to generate an additional critical dice against the opponent's defense, up to a maximum of thrice the pokemon's Aim level. If Aim is reduced to the negatives, roll an additional time for each negative stage, and pick the lowest. A ranged attack that is defeated by Dodge or Defense by a threshold of 6 or more applies no onslaught penalty.
- Efficiency: Determines how good a pokemon is at economizing its strength and spending its energy efficiently. Each level of efficiency adds a 24 / 20 / 16 / 12 / 8% chance for a pokemon to not drop an energy level when it otherwise would have. Pokemon whose Energy Level has fallen to 0 / 4 are treated as having 0 Efficiency.
- Evasion: Determines how good a pokemon is at dodging incoming attacks, both up close and from afar. Each level of evasion adds +2 or +20% to a pokemon's Speed-granted dodge value, whichever is higher.
- Melee: Determines how good a pokemon is at hitting its target when attacking up close. Each level of melee turns five hit dice into guaranteed successes when attacking at melee range. If the number of automatic successes exceeds the number of dice rolls, the excess are converted to hit dice. For each hit success that exceeds the opponent's dodge, there is a 50% chance to generate an additional critical dice against the opponent's defense, up to a maximum of thrice the pokemon's Melee level. If Melee is reduced to the negatives, roll an additional time for each negative stage, and pick the lowest. A melee attack that is defeated by Dodge or Defense by a threshold of 6 or more and has no other attacks between it and the opponent's next attack action, grants the opponent +1 Speed on their next attack again against the attacker if nothing more successful is launched in between.

- Fellowship: Represents how close the pokemon is to its trainer and the rest of the team. Each point of fellowship grants one wound, and higher levels allow for increased team tactics and stunts.

Mobility:
- Run: This pokemon can move along the surface of the ground. A pokemon without this movement type that is forced to remain on the ground will have its Evasion Skill reduced by 1, and incur a -2 penalty to Dodge and Defense.
- Swim: This pokemon can swim along the surface or underwater. Grants a bonus to dodge if fighting on a battlefield with water deep enough to dive, and negates this bonus on an opponent. A pokemon that only has this form of movement suffers a -1 Efficiency penalty when fighting on land, due to having to generate water to swim in. Pokemon that do not have this movement type that are immersed in water have their Speed penalized by -2, and Evasion reduced to 0. A pokemon that has this movement type and whose normal typing would cause them to be weak to Water will be neutral to Water instead.
- Burrow: This pokemon can burrow beneath the surface of the ground. The user ignores one point of Aim or Melee reduction from vision-blocking abilities against grounded targets, and gains +2 Defense against ranged attacks that don't automatically attack both opponents. This bonus is reversed into a penalty against tremor-based moves.
- Hover: This pokemon hovers above the ground with a relatively low height ceiling. Grants a +1 bonus to dodge, doubled against Ground-type attacks, while negating this bonus (except against Ground-type moves) against an opponent that also has it. Flying-types with this movement form are only resistant to Ground.
- Fly: This pokemon can fly freely through the air. Grants a +2 bonus to dodge, doubled against Ground-type attacks and contact moves if the user has not used or been hit by any contact attacks this round, while negating Hover and Fly bonuses (except against Ground-type moves) against an opponent that has them. In a tightly enclosed area such as a low building or cave, this movement type is reduced to Hover. Flying-types require this movement form to be immune to Ground.

Condition:
- Wounds: A pokemon has a number of wounds determined by its Stamina, plus one for every level of Fellowship and another for weighing 100 kilograms and every order of magnitude above that. Losing wounds will cause a pokemon to suffer penalties to Speed, Toughness and Evasion. Pokemon will recover their wounds in between battles without need to visit a center. A pokemon that has been reduced to zero wounds will faint, and will be unable to battle until given medical care at a pokemon center.
- Energy Levels: A pokemon has four energy levels, which are spent executing techniques in battle. A greater level of stamina allows for the use of more and higher-ranked techniques before dropping an energy level. Dropping energy levels will cause a pokemon to suffer penalties to Power, Speed, Aim and Melee. Pokemon can recover their energy by being withdrawn from battle, and energy levels will be restored at a rate of one per two rounds of combat undergone. A pokemon that has been reduced to its last energy level can't use moves with a higher rank than its Stamina. A pokemon reduced to an energy level of zero can still fight, but can only use D-Rank moves with no stunts, may not be able to fly, hover or burrow, and immediately loses all power-ups and has maneuver bonuses reduced to half.
- Status Effects: Status effects are negative effects with a set duration localized to the pokemon that they are afflicting. Attributes, skills and other properties can all be penalized by statuses applied by enemy attacks. Attribute penalties recover at a rate of one tick per two rounds withdrawn, while skill and other penalties are immediately recovered unless otherwise noted. If the pokemon has been put to sleep, each round switched out counts as 1 round against the sleep timer.
- Onslaught Penalties: A pokemon on the field takes a cumulative -1 penalty to Dodge for each attack that is aimed at it, and an additional -1 to Defense for each attack that hits, lasting until the end of the round. Some moves may apply more onslaught penalties or less. Ranged attacks do not apply onslaught penalties if they are defeated by Dodge / Defense respectively by a threshold 6 or more.

Moves:
- Contact and Ranged: Contact moves gain accuracy from the Melee skill, and ranged moves gain it from Aim. All ranged moves are able to attack both opponents in a double battle, but incur a -2 penalty to Hit and damage, unless otherwise specified.
- Friendly Fire and Recoil: Attacks that hit allies do not apply attack pattern bonuses, and always allow for Defensive Reactions even if they would otherwise be too fast. Recoil damage does not apply same-type attack, offensive trainer specialist bonuses, or onslaught penalties. The innate Defense bonuses from the Defend maneuver and non-powerup Defense increasing moves do not apply against recoil damage, but all other Defense bonuses do, and the user's Wound penalties are ignored.
- Power-Ups and Status: No more than a single instance of a particular power-up or status move can be active on a pokemon at a time, save for statuses that vary intensity based on the number of Wounds dealt, and Defense / Dodge bonuses from non-power-up personal support moves cannot stack with each other.
- Clashing: Two moves that are used on the same tick may clash, with the loser being negated while the winner goes on to continue the attack. The winning move of the clash incurs a -1 Power and Speed penalty for every one of the following conditions that applies: the loser is the same rank, the loser is of a higher rank, the loser has a defensive type advantage, the loser has an offensive type advantage, the winner wins by a damage margin of 3 or less, the winner wins by a damage margin of 6 or less. Contact attackers may attempt to dodge before a clash, applying their Evade reaction if present, and will avoid the clash if they succeed or allow the opponent the opportunity to generate Critical Dice if they are hit. Ranged attackers who lose a clash have a chance to dodge the winning attack. When two contact attacks tie in a clash, each pokemon will be hit by the opposing move. When two ranged attacks tie in a clash, they may generate an explosion that combines the strengths of the attacks, and reduces its damage depending on the Dodge score of the two attackers. Normal-type and Fighting-type moves do not clash with Ghost-types, unless the immunity has been nullified. Ground-type moves clash neutrally against Flying.
- Initiating a Grapple: Moves that initiate a grapple force the attacker and victim to make an opposed control roll using Power, modified by Melee. The attacker gains a +1 advantage to the result for every 2 threshold hits, up to a maximum bonus equal to the attacker's Melee skill, and the side that is heavier gains an additional +1 if it weighs twice as much as the other and an additional +1 for every x3, then x4, x5 etc. weight multiplier it has over the other. Weights for flying and hovering pokemon are considered to be halved.
- Grappling: If the attacker wins, they initiate a grapple, which lasts a number of rounds equal to the number of threshold successes during the control roll. When grappling, the grappler incurs -1 to Evasion, and all of its Aim-based attacks against the grappled opponent will use Melee and be treated as contact instead. The victim of the grapple has Evasion reduced to 0, suffers -1 to Power, Speed, Toughness, and all remaining Skills, cannot target other opponents with Melee attacks, and loses all non-standard movement types, move-based Priority bonuses, and concealment effects, and cannot be grappled by a different effect until the first one ends. If the attacker is swimming or burrowing and the target cannot do the same, and the appropriate substrate is available in sufficient quantities to immerse them, then attribute penalties to the victim are applied to Stamina as well at the cost of 2 rounds of the grapple. Outside ranged attacks used by others against either pokemon will also attack the other with no Criticals and applying their penalty to attacking multiple opponents. Each round the grappler may commit attacks to deal the move's damage, costing no additional energy. The grappler can also use other attacks against its victim or other targets, but each rank of the attack uses up 1 round of the grapple. Each time the grappler takes a stage of onslaught penalties, takes Wounds, or drops a Wound level, it forfeits one round of the grapple. These penalties do not apply for moves where the user is not committed to the grapple. The grapple victim's attacks trigger any negative effect originating from the grappler that triggers on contact. Ghost-type pokemon automatically escape grapples at the end of the round.

Types: Normal, Fighting, Flying, Poison, Ground, Rock, Bug, Ghost, Steel, Fire, Water, Grass, Electric, Psychic, Ice, Dark, Dragon, Fairy.

- Type Advantages: When a pokemon is struck by an attack that is super-effective against it, all rolls of 1 within the damage dice pool will be re-rolled. When a pokemon is struck by an attack that is super-effective against two of its types, all rolls of 1 and 2 within the damage dice pool will be re-rolled. An attack that is not very effective will re-roll all results of 6 within the dice pool, and an attack that is not very effective against both types will re-roll results of 5 and 6. Automatic successes are also subjected to type interaction rolls, and will flip automatically upon hitting the target numbers.

Pokemon can either be of the animal or elemental morphology types. Elementals cannot be afflicted by status effects from moves of a not very effective type, and are more resistant to their strengths and weaker to their weaknesses. Type advantage rolls against elementals will be re-rolled until the triggering numbers no longer appear.

Pokemon with a type immunity against another type gain the benefits of single-resistance. In addition, they will substitute the sum of their highest and lowest attributes, modified by Evasion or +60%, whichever is higher, for Toughness when receiving a damage or false damage-dealing attack. Flying types that are not airborne do not have any resistance to ground. Attacks facing type immunity do not benefit from onslaught penalties, and do not apply them and are treated as if they had missed if they fail to inflict Wounds. Immunity automatically succeeds in protecting against collateral damage from attacks by allies.

- Same-Type Attack Bonus: A pokemon using an attack that is the same type as one of its own converts 1/6 of the native damage dice pool into automatic successes. The contributions of maneuvers and Critical Dice do not count for this calculation.

Trainer Specialties: Trainer specialties once learned affect every pokemon on hand. Trainer specialties are carried over during switches.
- Type Expert: Select one type. All attacking moves of this type gain +2 automatic successes to Wound, counting as automatic successes from same-type attack bonus for all effects that interact with that. Can teach moves of B-Rank and above from this type to pokemon that do not learn them as part of automatic progression, but the move in question must still be learnable by the species. Can set a goal to search for pokemon of this type to guarantee a find if it is available in the area being traversed, with a higher likelihood of coming across a more highly evolved individual. Cost: 500 experience for the first, 1000 experience for every one afterwards, reduced to 700 if the switch partner has it.
- Type Specialist: Select one type that you have Type Expert in, and at least three pokemon with this type. The more trained pokemon that you have of this type, the more powerful the bonus will be. Pokemon with Abilities that grant STAB in a type, resistances of a type, additional learnable moves from a single type, the ability to change into a type, or have the type or a close synonym in their name, also count as a pokemon of this type. For more details, see below. Cost: 1000 experience for the first, 2000 experience for every one afterwards, can have as many as desired.
- Attribute Expert: Select one attribute. All on hand pokemon have +1 to this attribute. Cost: 1000 experience for the first, 2000 experience for the second, can only have two.
- Skill Expert: Select one skill. If Aim, hit dice from Aim-based moves succeed on a 3. If Evasion, the user gains a Defense bonus equal to its Evasion. Defense bonus does not stack with the natural bonus from the Defend reaction. If Efficiency, and a single move causes multiple Efficiency rolls, reduce the number of rolls by 1. If Melee, hit dice from Melee-based moves succeed on a 3. Cost: 1000 experience for the first, 2000 experience for the second, can only have two.
- Attack Expert: Select one attack pattern. This attack pattern may be used in tandem with any other attack pattern during any attack action, allowing for that attack action to be modified by the bonuses / penalties of both, canceling at a 1-to-1 ratio if directly opposed. Cost: 2000 experience, can only have one.
- Defense Expert: Select one defensive reaction. This defensive reaction executes at +4 Priority. Cost: 1000 experience, can only have two.
- Move Expert: Select one attacking move of B-Rank or lower. Any pokemon that knows this move can use it with same-type attack bonus. Can teach this move even without its type expert. Cost: 2000 experience, can only have one.
- Combo Expert: Select one attacking move of B-Rank or lower. Any pokemon that knows this move can form combinations with it and other compatible B-Rank or lower moves. Can teach this move even without its type expert. Combination moves cannot be further combined by their user alone. Cost: 2000 experience, can only have one.

Type Specialist:
- E-Rank: Grants +1 automatic damage success to attacks of the specialty type for every trainer class above Nugget, counting as automatic successes from same-type attack bonus for all effects that interact with that. Requires 3 qualifying pokemon, each with at least 1 taught Merit, and at least 3 taught Merits total between them.
- D-Rank: Grants +1 hit dice to attacks of the specialty type for every trainer class above Nugget. Requires 4 qualifying pokemon, each with at least 1 taught Merit, and at least 5 taught Merits total between them.
- C-Rank: Grants a +2 Defensive Reaction Priority when defending with moves of the specialty type. Requires 5 qualifying pokemon, each with at least 1 taught Merit, and at least 8 taught Merits total between them.
- B-Rank: All pokemon resist the specialty type by one stage, does not grant elemental immunity to status if none existed before. Any pokemon that can learn at least one move of the specialty type will be able to learn at least one additional move, biasing similar moves, non-natural moves, and support moves. Requires 6 qualifying pokemon, each with at least 2 taught Merits, and at least 13 taught Merits total between them.
- A-Rank: Abilities that grant same-type attack bonus for the specialty type benefit from Specialist. Any pokemon that cannot learn any moves of the specialty type will be able to learn at least one move that most closely resembles moves that they can naturally learn. Requires 7 qualifying pokemon, each with at least 2 taught Merits, and at least 21 taught Merits total between them.
- S-Rank: Unlocks a type-dependent bonus for the entire team. Requires 8 qualifying pokemon, each with at least 3 taught Merits, and at least 34 taught Merits total between them.

S-Rank Type Bonuses:
Normal "Diversity": Damage-dealing attacks may resolve damage against dual-typed targets as either of their component types if this would be more advantageous. Statuses are still resisted if the target is normally a resistant elemental.

Fighting "Form": The type's form bonuses will stack with one another over the course of a round. Contact moves that are not of the Fighting-type will grant / receive bonuses if they precede or follow a Fighting-type move, and are the appropriate type of attack.

Flying "Precision": Critical Dice benefit from STAB.

Poison "Reaction": All moves that inflict statuses count as poisoning the target for purposes of triggering additional Wounds on an already poisoned target.

Ground "Onslaught": Attacks that reduce damage dice instead of missing completely knock opponents prone if they cause Wounds (treat as grappled) for a number of Priority ticks equal to the target's instances of onslaught penalties.

Rock "Momentum": Status effects applied by damage-dealing moves go quiescent when expiring or removed if the target does not switch out. These quiescent effects will be renewed if the target is Wounded by a move of the same type as the one that originally applied the status, using the new move's damage if appropriate.

Bug "Specialization": Attacking moves with effects that depend on a power-up, field effect, penalty or status that the move itself cannot apply have these effects doubled if applicable.

Ghost "Malediction": If a status effect applied by your pokemon expires, it causes all other status effects on that target to increase numerical penalties by 1 and trigger their expiration effects immediately if any while still remaining active.

Steel "Tempering": If the user takes penalties to Defense or Dodge from status effects, Wound penalties, or the user's own Abilities or power-ups, including indirectly from Attribute or Skill penalties, it will reverse these penalties for one round during the round after they expire. If a penalty is reduced without expiring entirely, the user gains a bonus equal to the magnitude of reduction. If the reduction was caused by time spent switched out, the user benefits on the first round after switching in.

Fire "Consumption": Attacks re-roll their damage dice with no automatic successes once for every Energy Level that they cause the user to drop. Successes are tested against the opponent's Defense, and threshold successes are added to Wounds dealt, and trigger Wound-based secondary effects. Resolve penalties on the defender after all rolls are completed.

Water "Adaptability": All pokemon that do not have elemental weakness to water gain the swim movement type. Pokemon that already swim ignore penalties for not having access to water, and gain +2 Speed in water.

Grass "Vitality": A pokemon that is reduced to half Wounds or below, but not knocked out by an attack will gain 3 ablative Wounds. This can occur more than once if a pokemon that has already benefitted from this heals itself above half Wounds.

Electric "Storm": Moves with additional hits and effects that cause moves to deal additional hits double any hits after the first. Additional instances of damage granted by this cause half Wounds.

Psychic "Time": Each pokemon may, once per battle when it is using two attack actions, switch the Priorities of the two actions.

Ice "Suppression": Reduces the attack and defense Priorities of opponents' actions by 1 for every status effect or point of attribute status penalty that is on them.

Dark "Retribution": Damage-dealing moves that lose power or are interrupted when the enemy hits or Wounds the user no longer do so. Instead, they lose half their damage dice on normal usage, and gain a quarter of it back each time the user is Wounded during the build-up, up to a maximum of their original damage dice value. The Judicious maneuver, if used on the second attack action, may cause the first attack in its action to qualify for this bonus, reducing damage dice to 0.75x (round up) and having a maximum damage dice bonus of 1.5x (round down) the original value with the buildup time being the gap between the end of the first and the beginning of the second attack actions.

Dragon "Overwhelm": Each pokemon gains +1 to any attribute that is higher than that of an opponent whom it attacks or defends against for interactions with that opponent.

Fairy "Equivalence": If an opponent derives a benefit from a move or ability effect of yours, the next time they would gain or refresh that benefit, it is rendered as a malus of equal magnitude.

Merits: Merits are taught to a single pokemon, which retains it no matter who is commanding. Each pokemon may only have one of each merit. Each merit costs 1000 experience to teach. Merits that modify Attributes and Skills that are taught to the first stage of an evolving pokemon may have their specific emphases changed upon evolution.
- Ace: This pokemon is especially powerful, and has +2 to a single chosen attribute, and +1 to each of the three others. May be taught a second time, but this will only boost the primary attribute to +3, and another one to +2.
- Prodigy: This pokemon has a special aptitude for certain skills. Subtract 1 point from a skill, then add 3 points to skills of your choice, with no more than 2 points going into a single skill. Skills cannot be raised above S-Rank (5) by this merit.
- Specialist: This pokemon is especially proficient at wielding one of its types. Choose one of that pokemon's types; attacks of that type have 1/3 of their damage dice rendered as automatic successes instead of 1/6.
- Mitigation: This pokemon is able to effectively handle attacks from one of its weaknesses, and has toughened its body as a side effect. Choose one type. The pokemon's unmodified base Defense against attacks of that type is equal to its lowest attribute plus its highest if that would be better, it clashes against attacks of that type with neutral effectiveness at worst, and it no longer counts as having that weakness for interactions with Abilities, penalties applied by weather, or terrain effects of that type. Additionally, this pokemon gains +2 to Defense, or a bonus to Defense equal to half the difference between unmodified base Defense and its highest unmodified Attribute (round up) when using Toughness to determine Defense.
- Alteration: Subtract 4 points from any of the pokemon's attributes, and add 6 back in. No more than 3 points can be added to or subtracted from any single attribute.
- Ability: The selected pokemon gains a second ability from a list of abilities available to its species.
- Foundation: This pokemon has had more of its innate potential unlocked through its connection to its trainer. Can only be taught to pokemon that cannot learn Ace. Gain +1 to two Attributes.
- Signature Move (also classified under Moves): Choose a known offensive move. This move gains same-type attack bonus, and benefits from Specialist if that merit is learned. If the user already has same-type attack bonus on a selected move, the trainer may choose at the time of instruction to have the move's Energy cost be reduced by one grade, or have the move re-roll the hit roll on a miss. A Signature Move can be rendered into a Combination utilizing it as one of the components, freeing up the Signature merit for further use.
- Combination (also classified under Moves): Choose two attacking moves of A-Rank or lower that are known by the selected pokemon. If these two moves can be combined, the pokemon can use them as a single move from now on. Combination moves cannot be further combined with other attacking moves by their user alone.
- Power Attack (also classified under Moves): Choose one known damage-dealing move that is B-Rank or lower. This move can now be optionally used at an A-Rank level, increasing its effects commensurately.
- Power Master (also classified under Moves): Choose one known power-up move that is B-Rank or lower. This effect is now permanently active without consuming Energy or actions, with single-use effects activating upon every switch-in. Moves that pay Energy multiple times per round ignore one payment per round. Power-up removing effects still function against this, removing its effects until the pokemon switches out or restores the move's effects by actively using it. B-Rank moves that are selected for this must not have any negative effects, and mastering a B-Rank requires the pokemon to sacrifice one point of Efficiency (may not be done if it already has no Efficiency).

Moves: Can teach moves of C-Rank and below without a specialty, or moves of any rank that a pokemon's prior stage learns naturally if its evolution was not a stone evolution.
- Costs: D-Rank - 100 experience. C-Rank - 200 experience. B-Rank - 400 experience. A-Rank - 1000 experience. S-Rank - 2500 experience.
